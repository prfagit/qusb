This stick is a vault. Not a backup of one. The vault.

Nothing here phones home. Nothing here needs install.
The program lives in bin/. The secrets will live in qusb/.

Open a terminal in this stick's folder. Below, QUSB stands for the
program for your machine, and <STICK> for this stick's mount path.

  macOS (Apple silicon)   bin/qusb-macos-arm64
  macOS (Intel)           bin/qusb-macos-x64
  Linux (x64)             bin/qusb-linux-x64
  Linux (arm64)           bin/qusb-linux-arm64
  Windows (x64)           bin\qusb-windows-x64.exe

macOS/Linux may need: chmod +x bin/qusb-*

THE GOLDEN RULES
----------------
1. Write the 24 words on paper. Ink. Twice, two places.
2. Never photograph them. Never type them into anything but qusb.
3. A vault key signs once. After that it is spent. This is the point.
4. Use each address once. Never send to an address you have already
   spent from: anyone can replay its old signature and move what
   lands there to the old recipient.
5. The classic lane is quantum-exposed. Treat it as a hot pocket,
   not a safe.
6. Eject the stick safely before you unplug it, every time.
7. Lose the stick: the words restore everything.
   Lose the words: nothing does.

1. CHECK THE PROGRAMS
---------------------
  QUSB verify

Every file must say "ok". This catches a damaged copy. It cannot
catch a stick someone tampered with on purpose: for that, compare
bin/SHA256SUMS with the release page on GitHub.

2. CREATE THE VAULT (once, on a machine you trust, ideally offline)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  QUSB init <STICK>

It shows 24 words once, asks for three of them back, then asks for
a passphrase. The passphrase unlocks this stick. The words rebuild
it if the stick is lost.

3. RECEIVE
----------
  QUSB receive

Shows your next unused vault address and a QR code. Send SOL to it
from any wallet or exchange. It also shows the classic address.

  QUSB status

Lists every vault and its balance. No passphrase needed.

4. PUT A LITTLE SOL ON THE CLASSIC ADDRESS
------------------------------------------
Sending costs a network fee, and a vault cannot pay it. Send about
0.002 SOL to the classic address from step 3. The network makes it
keep about 0.0009 SOL; the rest covers a few hundred sends. Skip
this if you will pay fees from another wallet's keypair file.

5. SEND, THE SHORT WAY (one online machine)
-------------------------------------------
  QUSB send --vault 0 --to <ADDRESS> --amount 1.5

Use --amount max to send everything. qusb shows what will happen and
waits for you to type yes. If you send only part, the rest moves to
your next vault, shown as "refund": vault 1 here. Vault 0 is then
spent for good. QUSB status shows where your SOL sits now.

6. SEND, THE COLD WAY (sign offline, broadcast online)
------------------------------------------------------
On the offline machine:

  QUSB sign --vault 1 --to <ADDRESS> --amount max

This writes qusb/sigs/vault1.qsig on the stick. The file holds no
secret. It can pay only the recipient written inside it, so it is
safe to copy to another drive or send to yourself. Keep a copy of
it somewhere off the stick until it has been broadcast.

On the online machine:

  QUSB broadcast qusb/sigs/vault1.qsig --payer stick

qusb checks the file, shows where the money goes and the maximum
fee, and waits for yes. --payer stick unlocks the stick on that
machine to pay the fee. To keep the stick away from the online
machine, copy the .qsig over and pay with a keypair file instead:
--payer keypair.json

If a send fails after signing, nothing is lost. The .qsig stays
valid: fix the cause and run broadcast again. If qusb says "status
unknown", look up the printed signature on a block explorer before
you retry.

A new recipient must receive at least 0.00089088 SOL, or the
network rejects the send.

7. EJECT
--------
Eject the stick in your system before you pull it out. qusb reminds
you whenever it has written to the stick. A stick pulled too early
can lose the record of which vaults are spent.

8. LOST THE STICK
-----------------
On a new stick, online:

  QUSB init <STICK> --restore

Type the 24 words. qusb reads the chain, finds the vaults you have
spent and the ones that still hold SOL, and continues after them.
It believes what the RPC node says, so use one you trust. qusb
refuses a node that does not keep full history.

Offline it cannot know which vaults are spent, so you must tell it:

  QUSB init <STICK> --restore --next-vault 20

Use a number above every vault you ever used. Vaults below it stay
locked until you go online and run:

  QUSB rescan

It lists the vaults it is about to unlock and waits for you to type
yes.

If you signed a .qsig and never broadcast it, the chain does not
show it. If you still have a copy of that .qsig, broadcast it.
Otherwise empty that vault in a single send --amount max and never
fund it again.
